Model: two discs (10 mm dia, 1 mm thick) electrically in parallel, cable 100 pF, input resistance 1 GΩ. Constants are typical textbook values; real sensors vary.

The physics

Q = d · F (charge, d = piezo coefficient in pC/N)
C = ε₀·εr·A / t
V = Q / (C + C_cable)
V(t) decays as e^(−t/RC)

Squeezing the crystal displaces its positive and negative charge centres, so opposite faces gain opposite charge. Two discs wired in parallel double the charge. Quartz has a small d but is very stable; PZT ceramics give about 100× more charge.

How it works, step by step

  1. Force acts on the seismic mass / force plate.
  2. The plate presses the stack of piezo discs between electrodes.
  3. Mechanical stress shifts charge inside the crystal lattice (direct piezoelectric effect).
  4. Surface charge appears on the electrodes; the centre electrode is the output.
  5. A charge or voltage amplifier converts it to a usable signal, which decays if force is constant.

Applications

  • Accelerometers and vibration monitors
  • Engine knock sensors in cars
  • Dynamic pressure and force sensors
  • Ultrasonic transducers, microphones, lighters
